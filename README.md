# QuantumCutoff: Limit Profiles of the Free Unitary Quantum Group

## Overview

This project provides a numerical exploration and visualisation of the **cutoff 
phenomenon** for Brownian motion on the free unitary quantum group $U_N^+$. 

The theoretical foundation is the asymptotic analysis of a convolution semigroup 
$(\psi_t^{(N)})_{t \geq 0}$ on $C(U_N^+)$ and its convergence toward the Haar 
state $h$ in total variation distance. The main result is that this convergence 
exhibits a **sharp cutoff** at time $t_N = \alpha_N(N \ln N + cN)$, with a 
limit profile described by a family of measures $\eta_c^r$ interpolating between 
shifted free Meixner laws and the semicircle distribution.

This repository implements the moment reconstruction and total variation distance 
computation numerically, and visualises the cutoff profile as $c$ and $r$ vary.

## Mathematical Context

### The Quantum Group $U_N^+$ and Its Subalgebra

The free unitary quantum group $U_N^+$ is defined as the universal unital 
$*$-algebra $C(U_N^+)$ [REF: Wang 1995] generated by the entries of an $N \times N$ unitary 
matrix, with no commutativity imposed. It carries a canonical Haar state (a trace) 
$h: C(U_N^+) \to \mathbb{C}$ — the unique unital, positive, tracial functional 
that is invariant under the quantum group operations [REF: placeholder].

Within $C(U_N^+)$, there is a distinguished subalgebra
$$
\mathcal{A} = \mathbb{C}\langle X^+, X^- \rangle
$$
generated as a unital algebra by two conjugate elements $X^+$ and $X^-$. The algebraic 
structure of $\mathcal{A}$ does not depend on $N$, so we suppress the $N$ index. 
The restriction of $h$ to $\mathcal{A}$ equips it with an inner product 
$\langle a, b \rangle = h(a^* b)$, and the following family is an orthonormal 
basis for $L^2(\mathcal{A}, h)$:
$$
\bigl\{ X_{\mathbf{n}}^\epsilon \bigr\}_{\mathbf{n},\, \epsilon}
$$
where $\mathbf{n} = (n_1, \ldots, n_p)$ is a tuple of positive integers and 
$\epsilon \in \{+,-\}$ is a sign. The basis elements are defined recursively by 
the conventions
$$
X_\emptyset = 1, \qquad X_1^\epsilon = X^\epsilon,
$$
and the multiplication rules
$$
X^\epsilon \, X_{\mathbf{n}}^\epsilon = X_{(1,\mathbf{n})}^\epsilon,
\qquad
X^\epsilon \, X_{\mathbf{n}}^{-\epsilon} 
= X_{\mathbf{n}^+}^\epsilon + X_{\mathbf{n}^-}^\epsilon,
$$
where for $\mathbf{n} = (n_1, \ldots, n_p)$:
$$
\mathbf{n}^+ = (n_1 + 1,\, n_2, \ldots, n_p),
\qquad
\mathbf{n}^- = 
\begin{cases}
(n_1 - 1,\, n_2, \ldots, n_p) & \text{if } n_1 - 1 > 0, \\
(n_2, \ldots, n_p) & \text{if } n_1 = 1.
\end{cases}
$$
There is also a commutative subalgebra
$$
\mathcal{A}_0 = \mathbb{C}[x], \qquad x = \frac{X^+ + X^-}{\sqrt{2}},
$$
on which an orthonormal basis is given by the Chebyshev polynomials of the second 
kind $x_m = P_m(x)$, defined by
$$
P_0 = 1, \quad P_1 = x, \quad x P_n = P_{n+1} + P_{n-1} \quad (n \geq 1).
$$
The restriction of $h$ to $\mathcal{A}_0$ coincides with the **semicircle 
distribution**
$$
\nu_{\mathrm{SC}} = \mathbf{1}_{|x| \leq 2}\, \frac{\sqrt{4 - x^2}}{2\pi}\, \mathrm dx,
$$
uniquely determined by the moment conditions 
$\nu_{\mathrm{SC}}(P_m) = \delta_{m0}$ for all $m \in \mathbb{N}$.

### The Brownian Motion and Its Spectral Data

The Brownian motion on $U_N^+$ is encoded by a convolution semigroup 
$(\psi_t^{(N)})_{t \geq 0}$ of states on $C(U_N^+)$. On the basis 
$\{X_{\mathbf{n}}^\epsilon\}$ of $\mathcal{A}$, it acts as:
$$
\psi_t^{(N)}\!\left(X_{\mathbf{n}}^\epsilon\right)
= P_{\mathbf{n}}(N)\,
\exp\!\left(
-t\!\left(\alpha_N \lambda_{\mathbf{n}}^{(N)} 
+ \beta_N \frac{\xi_{\mathbf{n}}^2}{N}\right)
\right),
$$
for some $\alpha_N,\beta_N\ge0$, where:
$$
P_{\mathbf{n}} = P_{n_1} \cdots P_{n_p}, \qquad
\lambda_{\mathbf{n}}^{(N)} 
= \frac{P_{\mathbf{n}}'(N)}{P_{\mathbf{n}}(N)}, \qquad
\xi_{\mathbf{n}} = \sum_i (-1)^{k_i + i},
$$
with $k_1 < k_2 < \cdots$ the ordered indices of the $j$'s for which $n_j$ is 
odd, and $\alpha_N, \beta_N > 0$ are scaling parameters of the semigroup.

---

### The Cutoff and Its Limit Profile

Assume $\beta_N \ln N / \alpha_N \to r \in [0, \infty]$ as $N \to \infty$. At 
the cutoff time
$$
t_N = \alpha_N(N \ln N + cN), \qquad c \in \mathbb{R},
$$
the state $\psi_{t_N}^{(N)}$, restricted to $\mathcal{A}_0$, converges 
asymptotically to the distribution $\eta_c^r$ defined by its moments against the 
Chebyshev basis:
$$
\int P_n\, \mathrm d\eta_c^r = e^{-cn}\, \mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{-r S_n^2}\right],
\quad n \in \mathbb{N},
$$
where $(S_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ is a $\pm 1$ random walk on $\mathbb{Z}$, and 
by convention $\mathbb{E}[e^{-\infty S_n^2}] = \mathbb{P}[S_n = 0]$.

**Decomposition of $\eta_c^r$.** The measure $\eta_c^r$ admits an explicit 
Lebesgue decomposition with respect to $\nu_{\mathrm{SC}}$:
$$
\mathrm d\eta_c^r = f_c^r\, \mathrm d\nu_{\mathrm{SC}} + \mathrm d\eta_{c,\mathrm{sing}}^r,
$$
where the absolutely continuous density is
$$
f_c^r(x) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{1}{e^{-2c}R_r^2 - x e^{-c} R_r + 1}\right],
\quad x \in (-2, 2),
$$
and the singular part is
$$
\eta_{c,\mathrm{sing}}^r(A)
= \mathbb{E}\!\left[\left(1 - e^{2c} R_r^{-2}\right)_+
\mathbf{1}_A\!\left(e^{-c} R_r + e^c R_r^{-1}\right)\right].
$$
Here $R_r = \cos T_r$ where $T_r \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 2r)$, with the conventions 
$T_0 = 0$ (so $R_0 = 1$) and $T_\infty \sim \mathrm{Unif}([0, 2\pi])$.

**Special case $r = 0$.** When $r = 0$ the measure reduces to a shifted free 
Meixner law [REF: placeholder]:
$$
\eta_c^0 
= \left(1 - e^{2c}\right)_+ \delta_{e^c + e^{-c}}
+ \frac{\mathrm d\nu_{\mathrm{SC}}(x)}{e^{-2c} - x e^{-c} + 1}
= \mathrm{Meix}^+(-e^{-c}, 0) * \delta_{e^{-c}}.
$$
More generally, $\eta_c^r$ is a mixing measure over the one-parameter family
$$
\frac{\mathrm d\nu_{\mathrm{SC}}(x)}{q^2 - xq + 1}
+ \left(1 - q^2\right)_+ \delta_{q + q^{-1}}
= \mathrm{Meix}^+(-q, 0) * \delta_q,
\quad q \in \mathbb{R}^*,
$$
mixed over $q = e^{-c} R_r$.

**Interpretation.** The parameter $c$ controls how far before or after the 
cutoff time the system is observed: $c \to -\infty$ corresponds to long before 
the cutoff (the state is far from equilibrium), $c \to +\infty$ to long after 
(the state has mixed). The parameter $r$ controls the regime: $r = 0$ gives pure 
free Meixner behaviour; $r > 0$ introduces sinusoidal Gaussian randomisation; 
$r = \infty$ gives the fully mixed regime. A sharp cutoff (in its full meaning) is proved only for 
$c > 0$.

## Computational Goals

The project pursues three numerical objectives, in increasing difficulty.

**Objective 1 — Visualise $\eta_c^r$.** 

Reconstruct the density $f_c^r$ on $(-2, 2)$ and the singular atom 
(when $c > 0$) from the moment formula 
$\int P_n\, \mathrm d\eta_c^r = e^{-cn}\mathbb{E}[e^{-rS_n^2}]$, for a range of 
$(c, r)$ values. Plot the cutoff profile 
$c \mapsto d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\eta_c^r, \nu_{\mathrm{SC}})$ to exhibit the sharp 
transition at $c = 0$ when $r > 0$ and the absence of a sharp transition when 
$r = 0$.

**Objective 2 — Approximate $\psi_{t_N}^{(N)}$ on $\mathcal{A}_0$.**

For finite $N$ and times $t_N = \alpha_N(N \ln N + cN)$, directly compute 
$\psi_{t_N}^{(N)}(P_m)$ using the spectral data $\lambda_{\mathbf{n}}^{(N)}$ 
and $\xi_{\mathbf{n}}$, and compare the resulting approximate density to 
$\eta_c^r$ as $N$ grows. This gives a numerical verification of the 
asymptotic convergence.

Note: the restriction to $\mathcal{A}_0$ is valid only in the regime $c \geq 0$ 
where $\eta_c^r$ is absolutely continuous with respect to $\nu_{\mathrm{SC}}$.
For $c < 0$ the singular part of $\eta_c^r$ is non-trivial and the restriction 
to $\mathcal{A}_0$ loses information.

**Objective 3 — Total variation on the full algebra $\mathcal{A}$.**

The total variation distance 
$d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\psi_t^{(N)}, h)$ is computed via
$$
d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\psi_t^{(N)}, h) 
= \sup_{p \text{ projection}} 
\left|\psi_t^{(N)}(p) - h(p)\right|.
$$
For $c < 0$, the maximising projection is not captured by $\mathcal{A}_0$ alone. 
The goal is to search for candidate maximising projections in the larger algebra 
$\mathcal{A}$ using the full basis $\{X_{\mathbf{n}}^\epsilon\}$, and 
numerically verify whether the $\mathcal{A}_0$-restricted distance underestimates 
the true total variation.
